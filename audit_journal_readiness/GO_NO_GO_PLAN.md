## 2. Highest-value research task — the go/no-go experiment

### E2. External-validity replication on one realistic multi-OD network

- **Research question.** Is the negligible decision value of per-context routing-policy
  selection a property of the toy three-corridor network, or does it persist on a
  realistic signalised network with multiple OD pairs and overlapping routes?
- **Why the present data cannot answer it.** All 12,960 runs share one OD pair, three
  disjoint corridors, a complete path catalogue and full compliance. Those are exactly the
  conditions under which a capacity-aware load-balancing policy is expected to dominate.
- **Design (minimum credible).**
  - Network: an open, citable SUMO scenario or an OSM extract of a real district, with
    ≥ 20 signalised junctions, ≥ 4 OD pairs and overlapping route sets. The student's own
    city is a reasonable choice if demand can be justified.
  - Policies: general-network versions of P1–P4 over a k-shortest-path set (k = 3–5) per
    OD. Implement and freeze them before any evaluation run, reusing the existing unit
    and integration test pattern.
  - Factors (reduced): demand 3 levels × penetration 3 × information lag 2 × incident 2
    = 36 contexts; 4 policies; 5 paired seeds → **720 runs**, plus a P3 ε-variant arm
    (180 runs) to test the tuning-versus-selection finding.
  - Analysis: the existing pipeline, unchanged, with a **cross-fitted** hindsight
    reference and SBS chosen on training folds. LOCO over contexts.
- **Primary outcome.** Cross-fitted SBS→VBS headroom as a percentage of the SBS mean
  journey time, plus the realised gain of the best selector on held-out seeds.
- **Pre-declared decision thresholds** (write them down before running):
  - **Negligible:** cross-fitted headroom < 0.5 % **and** best-selector realised gain
    < 0.5 %.
  - **Material:** cross-fitted headroom ≥ 2 % with a selector realising ≥ half of it on
    held-out contexts.
  - In between: **inconclusive**.
- **What each outcome means for the paper.**
  - *Negligible on both networks* → a two-network negative result: "per-context selection
    among established routing policies has little value; choose and tune the fixed policy".
    This is publishable as a focused paper. Drop the gate from the contribution list.
  - *Material on the realistic network* → the toy network was the reason the effect was
    small. Selection becomes a real problem, and the existing ladder and shift protocol
    become the method of a stronger paper.
  - *Inconclusive* → the question needs more than the time box allows; go to §5.
- **Expected cost.** Implementation is the main cost: about 2–4 weeks for k-shortest-path
  P1–P4 plus validation. Simulation: 900 runs at 1–5 core-minutes each is 15–75 core-hours,
  roughly 4–19 hours on 4 cores. Requires the student's approval before launch.
- **Stop rule.** If the general-network policies are not implemented and passing their
  tests by the end of week 4, stop (§5).

## 3. Should do (cheap, on the existing network)

### E1. Selection versus configuration
- **Question.** Is per-context policy *selection* worth anything once the fixed policy is
  *tuned*? And does per-context *configuration* of P3 (choosing ε by context) have more
  value than per-context choice among policies?
- **Why the present data cannot answer it.** ε was varied only on a 96-context,
  g/C = 0.5 subset with two alternative values, chosen after the main results. The
  16.66 s/veh gain is post hoc.
- **Design.** P3 at ε ∈ {0.3, 0.5} on all 648 contexts × 5 seeds = **6,480 runs**
  (≈3 h on 4 cores at the toy network's measured cost). Declare the analysis before
  running: (i) tuned-SBS chosen on training folds; (ii) headroom of policy selection
  against the tuned SBS; (iii) headroom of per-context ε selection.
- **Outcomes.** If tuning yields ≥ 10× the selection headroom on all 648 contexts, the
  paper's practical message becomes "tune, don't select". That is a sharper and more
  useful claim, but still single-network, so it does **not** replace E2. If tuning gains
  vanish on the full factorial, drop the claim.
- **Stop rule.** None needed; this is a half-day experiment that strengthens either outcome
  of E2.

## 4. Go criterion

Proceed to a journal submission only if **all** hold by week 6:
1. M1 is resolved and any overlap is disclosable with a genuine extension.
2. M2: the student owns and can defend the design.
3. M3 corrections are applied.
4. E2 is completed with a **negligible** or **material** outcome (not inconclusive).

Then rewrite the manuscript around the single contribution that survived. Lead with the
two-network decision-value result. Cite the closest prior work (Xie et al. 2014;
Ansótegui et al. 2018; Laroche et al. 2019; Sawarni et al. 2026; Kerschke et al. 2018).
Report the gate as a negative result, not a mechanism.

| Audit scripts in this directory | Reproducibility appendix |

**A sharper question the existing evidence points to, but cannot answer:**
*For urban route guidance, is context information better spent choosing among
established routing policies or configuring one policy?* The closest literature is
algorithm configuration versus selection (e.g. per-instance configuration in
AI/OR; not surveyed in depth here). The missing contribution is a controlled comparison
in traffic routing. The minimal experiment is E1 on two networks. This is only worth
pursuing if E1 shows a large tuning effect on the full factorial. Do not start it merely
because the current paper is weak.
