\section{Limitations}

\paragraph{The environment is synthetic and the study claims no external
validity.} The network is a controlled instrument built to isolate mechanisms,
not a model of any city. Every result is scoped to it. What is portable is the
method rather than the numbers: the descriptors are dimensionless, so a boundary
is stated as a degree of saturation and a controlled-flow share rather than as a
count of vehicles per hour, and both are measurable on a real network. No claim
of real-world validation is made anywhere in this paper.

\paragraph{The context is assumed observable.} The selector is given the
operating conditions. A deployed system would have to forecast them, and would
inherit the forecast's error on top of the selector's. Forecasting the context
is a separate problem and is not addressed here. The reported decision quality
is therefore an upper bound on what the same selector would achieve behind a
forecast.

\paragraph{One routed origin--destination pair.} Corridor demand is a single OD
pair choosing among three corridors. The four minor streets carry independent
demand through the same signals, which is what couples the routing decision to
traffic that is not being routed, but the decision itself is a three-way choice
on one OD pair. Many simultaneously-routed OD pairs, with the interactions
between their choices, is the clearest extension and is not covered by anything
reported here.

\paragraph{The uncertainty guarantee is conditional and partial.} Split
conformal coverage requires exchangeability, which covariate shift
breaks~\cite{shimodaira2000,tibshirani2019covshift}. The conformal gate is a
calibrated instrument in-distribution and a heuristic outside it; the support
gate is what addresses extrapolation; and neither detects concept shift --- a
context can sit comfortably inside the training support while the relationship
between context and outcome has changed. We report coverage only where its
assumption holds, and report gate behaviour rather than guarantees elsewhere.

\paragraph{Grid resolution bounds every boundary claim.} The experiment samples
a grid. A boundary is reported as a bracket between adjacent sampled levels and
never as a continuous threshold, even after refinement, because a grid cannot
locate a boundary more finely than its own spacing.

\paragraph{No causal identification.} Where a factor is associated with a change
of preferred policy, that is an association within a designed factorial, not an
identified causal mechanism. The factorial licenses the statement that the
preference differs across levels of a factor with the others held fixed; it does
not license a claim about why, beyond the mechanistic reasoning that motivated
the design, which is a hypothesis the experiment is consistent with rather than
a result it establishes.

\paragraph{Policy parameters are fixed points, not optima.} The admissibility
tolerance of the load-balancing policy and the dispersion weight of the
reliability-aware policy were frozen before the experiment and varied afterwards
only for sensitivity. Neither was optimised, and a better-tuned version of
either could change its standing. We report the sensitivity rather than claiming
the frozen values are the best available.

\paragraph{The emission model is a simulator component.} \CO{} is computed by
the simulator's HBEFA3 implementation for one vehicle class
\cite{krajzewicz2015emissions}. Fleet composition, cold starts and gradient are
not modelled, so C2 should be read as a consistent comparative index across
policies in this environment rather than as an absolute emissions estimate.

\paragraph{No monetary valuation.} No verified value of time, value of
reliability or carbon price was available for this study, so none is used. The
criteria are compared by Pareto dominance, and scalar sensitivity uses declared
normalised weight profiles. This keeps the study free of invented constants, but
it also means the paper cannot express a trade-off in currency, which is what an
operator would ultimately want.
