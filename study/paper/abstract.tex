\begin{abstract}
Navigation systems implement established routing objectives --- shortest
distance, dynamic travel time, capacity-aware load balancing, reliability-aware
routing --- but commit to one by design rather than by traffic condition. We
characterise that choice empirically. In \NumRuns{} SUMO runs over
\NumContexts{} controlled contexts spanning demand, signal timing, guidance
penetration, information lag, disruption and alternative capacity, with every
comparison paired under common random numbers, we separate two questions that
are routinely conflated: whether the preferred policy varies with context, and
whether exploiting that variation is worth anything.

It does vary. Three of four policies are strictly best in some context by a
seed-resolved margin, the winner map needs more than one factor, and
\NumParetoNonSingleton{} of contexts have a non-singleton Pareto set over
journey time, \CO{} and stopped delay --- with the reliability-aware policy
minimising stopped delay in \NumSpecCThreePct{} of contexts while almost never
minimising journey time. Exploiting it is worth \NumHeadroom\,s per vehicle
(\NumHeadroomPct{}), because the best fixed policy is near-dominant, with an
upside \NumAsymRatio$\times$ its downside; choosing the wrong fixed policy costs
\NumFixedWorstPenaltyPct{}. Within that margin, a selector trained to predict
the winner's identity is \emph{more accurate and more expensive} than the fixed
policy (\NumBOneTopOne{} vs \NumBZeroTopOne{} accuracy;
\NumBOneRegret\,s vs \NumBZeroRegret\,s regret), whereas one trained to predict
each policy's \emph{advantage} is less accurate and closes
\NumBFourGapClosed{} of the available gap. Adaptation helps in-distribution and
harms under every extrapolation and shift tested; a support-and-confidence gate
prevented all of that harm by abstaining, correctly, because the conformal
interval is wider than the effect.

The operational finding is that the decision worth making here is which fixed
policy to run and which criterion to optimise, not which policy to run in which
context --- and that this can be established before any model is built, by
comparing the best fixed policy with the retrospective best and both with the
noise floor of the same comparison. We introduce no routing, learning or
multi-criteria decision algorithm.
\end{abstract}
\textbf{Keywords:} routing-policy selection; traffic regimes; decision regret;
algorithm selection; microsimulation; distribution shift; selective prediction.
